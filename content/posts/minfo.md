Allergic to permanent addresses, Abhijith has been on the road since 2012, offering to help corporations and people in cities and roadsides looking for a quick fix to their identity woes. The 1981 Royal Enfield (older than he is, christened Greta) breaks down once every month like clockwork (or verymuch unlike the idea of clockwork), giving him enough time to get to know the places she (it?) breaks down at. He appreciates people ready to offer a ride and promptly disappoints them (and himself) in true nomad fashion.

He is a trained pixel-whisperer (can use most of the Adobe Suite without embarrassing himself) and fixer of bad kerning, skills he stumbled upon at an exposed brick building on the banks of the Sabarmati and later perfected at a similar-but-plastered isolated multistorey house in (erstwhile) Gurgaon. After some years trying to prove highschool geography lessons wrong, and overwhelmed by the amount of books he was accumulating in the rented house, he moved to the south of India. The geography lessons were proved right and the single-speed bicycle he had dragged along proved a wise choice of gear (ooh!\*). He tried farming seasonal vegetables on leased land and ended up with too many bittergourds on the dining table. He still believes there is a major philosophical lesson there, waiting to be put into words.

He is a collector of discarded Cinthol soap wrappers from the 1980s and believes graphic design hasn't progressed since.

\\* That ‘ooh!’ is for successfully putting ‘gear’ in a sentence discussing a single-speed bicycle.
